==== 2.2.4 Interface Requirements
===== Definition

In this project, _interface requirements_ #describe how users interact with the system to input, modify and view information related to their fitness activities. These focus on the boundary between the user and the system, specifying the actions users can perform and the information presented in response.#

#Interface requirements define the available user interactions without specifying implementation details or internal data structures.#

===== Interface Requirements

====== User Initialization

The system shall provide means for a real-world individual to create a user account #by entering required registration information#.

====== Recording Real-World Workouts

#The system shall provide an interface for a user to record a workout session that occurred in the real world.# 

====== Editing and Correction of Workout Data

The system shall provide means for a user to #view and# edit previously recorded Workout Sessions in order to correct incorrect or incomplete entries. 

====== Goal Initialization and Update

The system shall provide means for a user to #create, view# and update fitness Goals. 

====== Viewing Progress and History
#The system shall provide means for a user to view their recorded workout history and overall progress over time.#

====== Automatic Streak Synchronization

#The system shall display the user’s current streak or consistency indicator.
The system shall update the displayed streak or consistency indicator in response to changes in workout data.#

====== Example Usage Scenario

#Scenario 1: Late Logging and Editing a Past Entry#

#Alejandro finished a gym workout on Monday evening after classes. He got
home tired, ate dinner, and forgot to record the session immediately.
Later that night, he remembered that he wanted to keep his weekly history
accurate, so he opened the web application, logged into his account, and
navigated to the workout recording page. He selected Monday as the workout
date, chose "gym" as the workout type, entered the session duration, and
saved the entry. Upon submission, the system created a persistent internal
representation of that Workout Session under Alejandro's account and
recalculated progress and streak values derived from the updated workout
history.#

#The next morning, Alejandro realized that he had entered the wrong
duration because he forgot to include his cooldown. He reopened the
application, navigated to his most recent recorded session, selected the
edit option, corrected the duration, and saved the change. The system
updated the stored Workout Session, preserved its association with the same
user, and recalculated the dependent indicators so that the internal
representation remained consistent with Alejandro's real-world activity.#

#Scenario 2: Missed Day and Recovery After a Broken Streak#

#Ana had recorded workouts on Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday and had been
paying close attention to her weekly consistency. On Thursday, she stayed
late finishing coursework and did not work out. When she opened the
application on Friday afternoon to review her progress, she saw that her
previous streak had ended because a full expected day had passed without a
recorded workout. However, the rest of her weekly activity history was
still visible, so the missed day did not erase the effort she had already
made earlier in the week.#

#That evening, Ana decided to restart instead of giving up on the week. She
went to the gym, returned home, opened the application, and recorded a new
Workout Session with the correct date and workout type. The system stored
the entry, updated her weekly progress indicators, and reflected that even
though the old streak had been broken, the week still contained meaningful
activity. This scenario validates recovery after a non-happy-path event
rather than only ideal uninterrupted use.#

#Scenario 3: Invalid Input Rejected Before Persistence#

#Derek went for a run on Sunday morning and wanted to log it quickly before
starting the rest of his day. While filling out the form, he accidentally
selected a workout date in the future and left one of the required fields
empty. He submitted the entry anyway. The system rejected the submission
before persistence and displayed validation feedback indicating that
workout dates cannot be in the future and required fields cannot be empty.
No Workout Session was stored.#

#Derek corrected the date, completed the required information, and
submitted the form again. This time, the system accepted the data,
persisted the Workout Session under Derek's account, and updated his
history accordingly. This scenario validates that invalid interface input
is blocked before storage and that the user can recover immediately by
correcting the entry.#
