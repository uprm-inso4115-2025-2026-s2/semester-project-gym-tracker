==== 2.1.1 Domain Rough Sketch

#This subsection now shows both the raw elicitation input and the team's processed interpretation so the reader can see what participants actually said versus how the team abstracted it. Raw statements are labeled explicitly, and the interpretation that follows each group is also labeled explicitly.#

*#Raw stakeholder statements about tracking and friction#*

* #""When I look back at last week, I remember that I worked out, but I do not remember which exercises I did on each day or how long the session lasted.""#
* #""If logging makes me enter too many details, I stop doing it after a few days even if I am still going to the gym.""#
* #""For me, the minimum useful record is the day, the total duration, and what kind of workout I did.""#
* #""Sometimes a session is mostly lifting, sometimes it is cardio, and sometimes it is a mix, so I want to remember what made that session different.""#
* #""I do not always repeat the exact same exercises, but I usually want to note the main ones I did so I can compare sessions later.""#
* #""There is a difference between knowing I went to the gym that day and knowing what I actually did during that workout.""#
* #""Tracking should be quick enough that it does not feel like another workout by itself.""#
* #""If the app feels complicated, I stop logging even when I am still trying to stay consistent.""#

*#Team interpretation of tracking and friction#*: #In our brainstorming sessions, we explored the broader issue of maintaining consistency in gym routines and how people experience motivation over time. A recurring pattern was the challenge of remembering and tracking what was done during a workout. Participants described not recalling how many times they went to the gym each week or what exercises they performed during their last session. This led to a discussion about how people currently track their workouts: some use note-taking apps, some rely on memory, and others do not track at all. The group also emphasized that tracking should remain lightweight, since excessive effort or complexity could discourage regular use.#

*#Raw stakeholder statements about consistency and streaks#*

* #""Seeing several active days in the same week makes me feel like I am staying on track, even if they are not consecutive.""#
* #""A streak is motivating because consecutive days make me feel disciplined, but missing one day should not make the rest of the week feel worthless.""#
* #""For me, consistency is about whether I keep showing up regularly over time, not only whether I never miss a day.""#
* #""Someone can be consistent without having a perfect streak, because they may still work out regularly across the week.""#

*#Team interpretation of consistency and streaks#*: #A significant portion of the conversation focused on consistency. The idea of maintaining a "streak" was repeatedly brought up as something that psychologically motivates people. The term "Duolingo-like" was used to describe how maintaining consecutive days of activity can encourage discipline. At the same time, concerns were raised about how reminders or notifications should be framed, since overly aggressive or judgmental messages might discourage rather than motivate.#

*#Raw stakeholder statements about weekly feedback and goals#*

* #"Weekly overview compared to goals."#
* #"Daily versus weekly goals."#
* #"No agreement on which works best."#
* #"Seeing progress motivates people."#

*#Team interpretation of weekly feedback and goals#*: #Another idea that emerged was the concept of end-of-week feedback. Some participants felt that having a weekly overview of activity compared to goals would increase accountability. The suggestion included showing whether weekly goals were achieved or how far off they were. The idea of daily versus weekly goals was discussed, with no final agreement on which structure works best.#

*#Raw stakeholder statements about nutrition and scope#*

* #"Nutrition tracking is related to fitness."#
* #"Including nutrition might broaden the focus."#

*#Team interpretation of nutrition and scope#*: #Nutrition tracking was mentioned multiple times. While some acknowledged that meal logging and calorie intake are related to fitness, many participants expressed concern that including detailed nutrition tracking might overly broaden the focus. Some suggested that nutrition could be treated as a separate concept or even a separate project. There was recognition that the A3 references nutrition, but uncertainty about whether it should be central to the current focus.#

*#Overall team interpretation#*: #Through these discussions, it became clear that the main concern is not advanced fitness analytics, but rather the human challenge of maintaining discipline, remembering to be active, and staying motivated over time.#
