== 4 Specific Class Topics

=== 4.1 Stakeholder Identification

==== Lecture Definition

According to the Domain Stakeholders lecture, stakeholders are individuals or groups who either:

* Have an *interest* in the domain, or
* *Depend* on the behavior, outcomes, or operation of the system.

The following stakeholder identification applies this definition to the fitness tracking system domain.

==== Initial Stakeholders

The following stakeholder groups were identifiable in the initial project phase based on domain analysis, requirements discussion, and usage scenarios. During evaluation, overlaps and classification boundaries were analyzed to ensure correctness and consistency with lecture-defined stakeholder concepts.

===== 1. Primary Users – Gym-Goers / Fitness Enthusiasts

*Interest:*
They seek to log workouts, monitor progress, maintain streaks, and improve consistency.

*Dependency:*
They depend on accurate tracking of Workout Sessions, Goal evaluation, and clear visibility of Progress and Streak status.

These users are directly affected by the system’s ability to represent and evaluate their fitness behavior.

===== 2. Beginner / First-Time Gym Users

*Interest:*
They want structure, clarity, and motivational reinforcement to build consistent habits.

*Dependency:*
They depend on simple tracking mechanisms, understandable feedback, and consistent progress summaries to avoid discouragement.

This stakeholder represents a specialization of Primary Users rather than a completely separate category. The distinction is maintained to reflect differences in usability needs and domain expectations.

===== 3. Goal-Oriented / Experienced Users

*Interest:*
They aim to achieve measurable targets and evaluate improvement over time.

*Dependency:*
They depend on reliable historical data, comparison of sessions, and meaningful goal evaluation outcomes.

They require that the system preserve data integrity and reflect performance trends accurately.

===== 4. System Administrators / Development Team

*Interest:*
They are responsible for maintaining system integrity, reliability, and alignment with requirements.

*Dependency:*
They depend on well-defined domain models, clear requirements, and predictable system behavior to implement and evolve the platform.

This group influences architectural and technical decisions.

===== 5. Platform Providers (e.g., Hosting / Backend Services)

*Interest:*
They require correct integration, API usage, and adherence to platform policies.

*Dependency:*
The system depends on them for authentication, persistent storage, and uptime.

Though indirect, they are critical external stakeholders due to infrastructure reliance.

===== 6. Future Trainers / Coaches (Potential Expansion Stakeholders)

*Interest:*
They may want insight into user consistency and performance trends.

*Dependency:*
They would depend on structured historical data and accurate summaries if future features allow coaching or review.

This stakeholder is not currently active within the system scope but is included to reflect potential domain expansion. Their inclusion supports domain awareness without introducing immediate requirements.

==== Domain vs. Implementation Stakeholders

Primary Users (including Beginners and Goal-Oriented Users) are *domain stakeholders* because their interest lies in fitness behavior, motivation, and observable outcomes such as consistency, streaks, and progress.

System Administrators and Platform Providers are *implementation stakeholders* because their concerns relate to system realization, infrastructure, and operational reliability rather than domain phenomena.

To strengthen traceability with domain terminology (Section 2.1.2), stakeholder dependencies can be mapped as follows:

* Primary Users → Workout Session, Streak, Progress, Goal
* Beginners → Workout Session, Weekly Activity Pattern, Consistency
* Goal-Oriented Users → Goal, Progress, compareSessions(...)
* System Administrators → Domain model consistency and system behavior
* Platform Providers → Account, persistence, and availability

This mapping ensures consistency between stakeholder needs and domain concepts while maintaining a clear separation between domain and implementation concerns.

==== Alignment with A3 and Project Scope

The identified stakeholders reinforce the project’s emphasis on:

* Workout logging as the core domain activity
* Progress and streak tracking as motivational mechanisms
* Weekly goal evaluation and accountability

Nutrition tracking was intentionally limited in scope based on stakeholder tension observed in early discussions. This reflects alignment with A3 priorities and prevents scope overexpansion.

==== Discovery of Additional Stakeholders

It is likely that additional stakeholders exist beyond those initially identified. To discover them, the team will:

* Conduct informal interviews or surveys with additional gym users.
* Review online fitness communities to identify overlooked needs.
* Revisit domain assumptions during each milestone.
* Analyze feature requests or feedback emerging during development.

When new stakeholders are identified, their interests and dependencies will be documented, and any resulting requirement changes will be reviewed for scope alignment before incorporation.

This iterative identification process ensures that stakeholder analysis evolves alongside the system while maintaining domain focus.

==== #4.1.1 Stakeholder Perspectives on the Fitness Consistency Problem#

#The fitness consistency problem is not experienced uniformly across stakeholder groups. The same gap between intention and behavior is interpreted differently depending on a user's experience level and motivation structure. The following perspective analysis maps each primary user stakeholder to their distinct view of the problem and the modeling implication that follows.#

[discrete]
===== #Beginner / First-Time Gym User Perspective#

#For the beginner, fitness inconsistency is fundamentally a problem of overwhelming complexity and lack of direction. The barrier is not laziness, it is not knowing where to start. Without a structured plan, sessions are skipped because deciding what to do feels harder than the workout itself. Each missed session reinforces the belief that fitness is not for them, creating a cycle of low confidence and avoidance. There is no visible progress to anchor to because they do not yet know how to measure small wins.#

#_Core perception: "I don't know what to do or if I'm doing it right, so I stop showing up."_#

#The system must prioritize simplicity and clear structure for this group. Onboarding flows, preset routines, and low effort logging paths directly address this stakeholder's barrier.#

[discrete]
===== #Intermediate / Experienced Fitness Enthusiast Perspective#

#For the experienced enthusiast, inconsistency is a problem of stagnation and diminishing motivation over time. They are not confused about what to do, they have done it before. When the body adapts and results slow down, the emotional reward of working out diminishes. Skipped sessions happen not from confusion but from a quiet feeling that it is not working anymore.#

#_Core perception: "I know what to do, but I've stopped seeing results, so it's hard to stay motivated to keep going."_#

#The system must surface measurable progress and historical trends for this group. Data that proves effort is still producing results, even when results are less dramatic, is what sustains their engagement.#

[discrete]
===== #Goal-Oriented User Perspective#

#For the goal-oriented user, inconsistency is a problem of disconnection between daily effort and long term outcomes. They have clear motivation and know what they want to achieve, but they struggle to maintain consistency when individual sessions feel arbitrary. Missing a session is not just a lost workout, it is a deviation from a plan, and without a system to log, review, and recalibrate, small disruptions cascade into abandoned goals.#

#_Core perception: "I know what I want to achieve, but I lose consistency when I can't see whether I'm actually on track to get there."_#

#The system must function as an accountability and progress record for this group. Goal tracking, session logging tied to targets, and deviation visibility are the critical features.#

[discrete]
===== #Comparative Perspective Summary#

[cols="1,1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| | #Beginner# | #Experienced Enthusiast# | #Goal-Oriented User#

| #Root of inconsistency#
| #Confusion and lack of direction#
| #Stagnation#
| #Disconnect from long-term goals#

| #Relationship to motivation#
| #Never fully established#
| #Was present, now fading#
| #Present but fragile without data#

| #Primary need of the tracker#
| #Simplicity and clear structure#
| #Progress visibility and variety#
| #Accountability and goal tracking#

| #How they interpret a skipped session#
| #"I don't belong here"#
| #"This isn't working anymore"#
| #"I'm falling behind on my goal"#
|===

[discrete]
===== #Notes on Perspective Alignment#

* #The Beginner and Goal-Oriented User perspectives are complementary — both require structured guidance, but for different reasons.#
* #The Experienced Enthusiast and Goal-Oriented User are partially aligned — both care about measurable progress, but one is motivated by seeing past results while the other is driven by future targets.#
* #The Beginner and Experienced Enthusiast represent opposing ends of a spectrum — one lacks confidence and knowledge, the other has knowledge but lacks continued motivation.#
