=== 4.3 Domain and Requirements Acquisition

#This section now separates *requirements acquisition* from *requirements elicitation* because they are related but not identical activities. Acquisition is the collection of raw information from stakeholders, existing systems, and other sources. Elicitation is the follow-up work of clarifying, comparing, resolving, and translating that raw information into domain concepts, requirements, and acceptance-relevant decisions.#

==== 4.3.1 Acquisition vs. Elicitation

#For this project, *acquisition* means gathering requirement-relevant material before interpreting it. Examples include questionnaires, interviews, review of existing fitness applications, and collection of stakeholder statements. The output of acquisition is evidence: answers, observations, examples, and domain terminology used by real stakeholders.#

#For this project, *elicitation* means taking that acquired material and asking the next engineering questions: what does the stakeholder actually need, where do answers conflict, which concepts belong in the core domain, and how should ambiguous behavior be resolved? The output of elicitation is not raw data; it is clarified requirements, scope decisions, candidate resolutions, and scenarios such as the FE-* and RT-* cases already documented in Section 2.2.3.#

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
|#Phase# |#Main purpose# |#Typical output in this document#

|#Requirements acquisition#
|#Collect raw domain and stakeholder information from named sources.#
|#Questionnaires, interview notes, app-review observations, and source-backed findings.#

|#Requirements elicitation#
|#Interpret and refine acquired information into explicit system decisions.#
|#Clarified requirements, inconsistency cases, candidate resolutions, and usage scenarios.#
|===

==== 4.3.2 Requirements Acquisition Activities

#The team uses the following acquisition activities to gather domain and requirement information from real sources instead of relying only on internal brainstorming:#

* #AQ1 - Structured questionnaire for gym-goers and other fitness-oriented UPRM students. This is the primary acquisition instrument in this section because it captures repeatable, comparable answers from real stakeholders.#
* #AQ2 - Targeted stakeholder micro-interviews (15-20 minutes each) with beginner gym-goers, mixed routine users, and habit-focused users to collect examples that need follow-up elicitation.#
* #AQ3 - Competitive review of existing fitness tracking tools to identify common logging fields, progress summaries, and friction patterns that stakeholders already recognize.#
* #AQ4 - Internal artifact review of the A3 problem framing, current personas, and earlier stakeholder notes so later acquisition remains aligned with the problem already documented in Sections 1 and 4.#

#Primary acquisition resources are therefore: direct stakeholders (UPRM gym-goers, beginner and intermediate users, casual runners), existing applications such as MyFitnessPal and Strava, and internal project artifacts already produced by the team.#

==== 4.3.3 Questionnaire AQ1: Gym-User Requirements Acquisition

#AQ1 is a structured questionnaire aimed at the stakeholder groups identified in Section 4.1, especially primary users, beginners, and goal-oriented users. It is designed for acquisition, not elicitation: its purpose is to collect stakeholder language, frustrations, priorities, and expectations before the team resolves them into requirement statements.#

#Target respondents:# #UPRM students and peers who currently attend a gym, run regularly, or are trying to build a workout habit.#

#Questionnaire AQ1 used for acquisition:#

[cols="1,5,3", options="header"]
|===
|#ID# |#Question# |#Requirement areas this question helps surface#

|#AQ1-Q1#
|#When you record a workout, which pieces of information do you always want to save, and which details feel optional or too time-consuming when you are in a hurry?# 
|#Low-Effort Logging, Essential Information Recording, Recording Real-World Workouts#

|#AQ1-Q2#
|#Think about the last time you forgot to log a workout immediately. What happened, how much later did you log it, and what should the system preserve or recalculate when you correct a past entry?# 
|#Persistent Storage and Retrieval, Editing and Correction of Workout Data, Automatic Streak Synchronization#

|#AQ1-Q3#
|#If your weekly goal is "work out 4 times this week", should two sessions on the same day count as one successful day or as two completed workouts? Explain what feels more natural to you and why.# 
|#Goal Evaluation, Weekly Aggregation, Performance Summary Representation#

|#AQ1-Q4#
|#What kind of weekly or monthly summary actually helps you decide whether you are making progress: attendance days, duration, exercise details, streaks, comparisons to past weeks, or something else?# 
|#Performance Summary Representation, Weekly Comparison, Viewing Progress and History#

|#AQ1-Q5#
|#What makes workout logging feel frustrating enough that you might skip it entirely: too many required fields, confusing terminology, slow input, needing nutrition data, or another reason? Please describe specific friction points.#

|#What personal account or profile information do you expect to manage in a fitness tracker so that your history, goals, and summaries still feel personal and useful over time?# 
|#User account management, Goal Initialization and Update, Viewing Progress and History#

==== 4.3.4 Elicitation Activities That Follow Acquisition

#After acquisition collects evidence, elicitation turns that evidence into precise engineering decisions. In this project, elicitation includes scenario walkthroughs, clarification questions, inconsistency analysis, scope narrowing, and candidate resolutions such as the FE-1 to FE-3 and RT-1 to RT-3 cases already documented in Section 2.2.3.#

#This separation matters because the questionnaire answers themselves are not yet requirements. For example, a participant saying "I often log my workout the next morning" is acquisition evidence. The elicitation step is the follow-up decision: should the streak depend on the workout date or the entry timestamp? That engineering resolution appears later as FE-1.#

#Similarly, a participant saying "I only want the basics when I am rushing" is acquisition evidence. The elicitation step is translating that into explicit requirements such as Low-Effort Logging and Essential Information Recording, while keeping optional detail fields available for users who want them.#

==== 4.3.5 Requirement Signals Surfaced by Questionnaire AQ1

==== 4.3.6 Incorporating New Findings

#When new acquisition findings affect the domain model or requirements, the team updates the document in the following order so acquisition and elicitation remain distinct:#

* #First, the new source is recorded as acquisition evidence (for example AQ1 questionnaire answers, interview notes, or app-review observations).#
* #Second, elicitation is performed on that evidence to clarify terminology, expose conflicts, and decide whether the finding changes the requirement set.#
* #Third, the affected domain, interface, or machine requirements are updated and the rationale is captured in the logbook and linked GitHub issue.#
* #Fourth, Section 4.4 traceability links are updated so the path from acquisition evidence to elicited requirement decisions remains visible.#
