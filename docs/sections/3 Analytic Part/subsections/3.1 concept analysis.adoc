== 3. Analytic Part

=== 3.1 Concept Analysis

This concept analysis transforms the rough sketch statements from the descriptive part into stable domain concepts, relationships, and terminology. The goal is to show how abstractions were derived (not just the final results).

==== 3.1.1 Concept Derivation: Activity Structure

===== From Activity Descriptions to Workout Session

.Rough sketch statements (side-by-side)
[literal]
[.changed]#A: "When I look back at last week, I remember that I worked out, but I do not remember which exercises I did on each day or how long the session lasted."#
[.changed]#B: "For me, the minimum useful record is the day, the total duration, and what kind of workout I did."#
[.changed]#C: "Sometimes a session is mostly lifting, sometimes it is cardio, and sometimes it is a mix, so I want to remember what made that session different."#

.Semantic analysis
* [.changed]#Statement A refers to a specific past occurrence of workout activity tied to a day and describable by internal content.#
* [.changed]#Statement B identifies a minimal unit of recording that includes when the activity happened, how long it lasted, and what kind of workout it was.#
* [.changed]#Statement C shows that one recorded occurrence can differ from another by composition or training type.#

.Common structure
* [.changed]#A bounded occurrence of workout activity that happens at a particular time and can be described by duration and internal content.#

.Abstraction
*WORKOUT SESSION*

.Definition
[.changed]#A specific occurrence of a Workout performed by a GymGoer at a particular time and on a particular Calendar Day.#

.Attributes
* [.added]#Calendar Day#
* Duration
* [.changed]#Training type#
* [.changed]#Exercises performed#

.Rationale
[.changed]#The statements do not merely mention activity in general; they refer to a recordable occurrence that has temporal identity, bounded duration, and describable internal structure. That supports the concept of a Workout Session rather than only a generic workout.#

===== Distinguishing Attendance Day from Workout Session

.Rough sketch statements (side-by-side)
[literal]
[.changed]#A: "Tracking the days a person worked out."#
[.changed]#B: "Tracking what was done during a workout."#

.Semantic analysis
* [.changed]#Statement A refers to whether workout activity occurred on a given Calendar Day.#
* [.changed]#Statement B refers to the specific structured activity that occurred.#
* [.changed]#A day with activity is not the same concept as the activity occurrence itself.#

.Terminology clash
[.changed]#Attendance and Workout Session were implicitly treated as interchangeable.#

.Resolution: introduce distinct concepts
* [.changed]#*ATTENDANCE DAY*#
* [.changed]#*WORKOUT SESSION*#

.Definitions
* [.changed]#*Attendance Day:* A Calendar Day on which at least one Workout Session has occurred.#
* [.changed]#*Workout Session:* A specific occurrence of a Workout performed by a GymGoer at a particular time on a particular Calendar Day.#

.Relationship
* [.changed]#One Attendance Day may correspond to one or more Workout Sessions.#

.Justification
[.changed]#This separation preserves semantic clarity by distinguishing temporal occurrence at the day level from the specific activity occurrence.#

==== 3.1.2 Concept Derivation: Temporal Patterns

===== From Repetition to Consistency

.Rough sketch statements (side-by-side)
[literal]
[.changed]#A: "Seeing several active days in the same week makes me feel like I am staying on track, even if they are not consecutive."#
[.changed]#B: "For me, consistency is about whether I keep showing up regularly over time, not only whether I never miss a day."#
[.changed]#C: "Someone can be consistent without having a perfect streak, because they may still work out regularly across the week."#

.Semantic analysis
* [.changed]#The statements refer to regularity of workout activity across a time span rather than to a single event.#
* [.changed]#They explicitly separate consistency from perfect consecutiveness.#
* [.changed]#The relevant pattern is frequency and distribution across time, not only whether each active day immediately follows the previous one.#

.Common structure
* [.changed]#Regular workout participation distributed across a Time Frame.#

.Abstraction
[.changed]#*CONSISTENCY*#

.Definition
[.changed]#A behavior describing how regularly Workout Sessions occur across a Time Frame, based on the distribution of Attendance Days and Missed Days in the relevant Workout History.#

.Relationships
* [.changed]#Consistency may remain high even when a Streak is broken.#
* [.changed]#Consistency is broader than Streak because it concerns regularity over a Time Frame rather than uninterrupted consecutiveness only.#
* [.changed]#Consistency can be interpreted through evaluateConsistency(...), which produces a Consistency Measure.#

.Justification
[.changed]#The statements support Consistency as a broader temporal pattern of regular participation, not merely as another name for a Streak.#

===== From Consecutive Days to Streak

.Rough sketch statements (side-by-side)
[literal]
A: "Maintaining a streak."
B: "Consecutive days of activity motivate discipline."

.Semantic analysis
* The term "streak" implies consecutiveness.
* Not all repeated activity is consecutive.

.Terminology clash
The terms "streak" and "consistency" were used interchangeably.

.Resolution: distinguish concepts
* *Consistency:* repeated activity (non-consecutive allowed)
* *Streak:* consecutive activity only

.Abstraction
*STREAK*

.Definition
A sequence of consecutive days containing workout activity.

.Relationship
* Streak is a specialized form of Consistency.

==== Concept Derivation: Missed or Failed Notifications Risk Analysis

:author: AlejandroMB05

== Overview

The notification system in the gym tracker application is intended to help [.changed]#GymGoers# maintain workout consistency and engagement by sending reminders, inactivity alerts, and workout-related updates. Because of this, missed or failed notifications present a significant risk to the effectiveness of the application.

This analysis identifies possible causes of notification failures, the impact these failures may have on [.changed]#GymGoers#, and considerations that may help reduce reliability issues in future development.

== Possible Causes of Missed or Failed Notifications

=== 1. Disabled Notification Permissions

[.changed]#GymGoers# may deny or later disable notification permissions on their device. If permissions are disabled, the application will not be able to deliver notifications even if triggers occur successfully.

*Potential Impact*

* [.changed]#GymGoers# may miss workout reminders
* Reduced workout consistency
* Lower user engagement

=== 2. Background Execution Restrictions

Some devices restrict applications from running in the background to save battery life. This may prevent scheduled notifications from being triggered properly.

*Potential Impact*

* Delayed reminders
* Missed inactivity alerts
* Inconsistent notification behavior across devices

=== 3. Incorrect Scheduling Logic

Improper handling of timestamps, time zones, or scheduling conditions may cause notifications to trigger at the wrong time or not trigger at all.

*Potential Impact*

* Notifications appearing too early or too late
* Duplicate reminders
* Reduced user trust in the system

=== 4. Application State Issues

Notifications may fail if the application crashes, closes unexpectedly, or loses synchronization with stored workout data.

*Potential Impact*

* Workout reminders not generated
* Missing progress-related notifications
* Loss of user confidence

=== 5. Device Connectivity Issues

Some notifications may depend on synchronization or cloud-based scheduling. Poor internet connectivity could interfere with notification updates or scheduling consistency.

*Potential Impact*

* Delayed updates
* Missed scheduled reminders
* Incorrect notification timing

== Recommendations

=== 1. Permission Awareness

The application should detect whether notification permissions are enabled and inform [.changed]#GymGoers# when notifications are disabled.

=== 2. Notification Validation

The system should validate scheduled notifications to ensure reminders are not duplicated or triggered after workouts are completed.

=== 3. Time Handling Consistency

Notification scheduling should consistently handle timestamps and time zones to reduce timing-related issues.

=== 4. Fallback Strategies

The system should consider fallback mechanisms for missed reminders, such as rechecking notification conditions when the application is reopened.

=== [.changed]#5. GymGoer-Controlled Notification Settings#

Allowing [.changed]#GymGoers# to configure reminders and notification behavior may reduce frustration caused by excessive or poorly timed notifications.

== Summary

Missed or failed notifications represent an important reliability risk for the gym tracker application. Notification failures can reduce user engagement, weaken workout consistency, and negatively affect the overall user experience.

The main risks identified include disabled permissions, background execution restrictions, scheduling errors, application state issues, and connectivity problems. Addressing these concerns early in the design process will help create a more reliable and [.changed]#GymGoer-friendly# notification system in future development.
