=== Modeling Domain Constraints and Invariants in the Fitness Tracking Domain

Alejandro E. López Rodríguez

Many aspects of the fitness tracking domain are governed by constraints that must
always hold true regardless of how activities are recorded or analyzed. These constraints,
commonly referred to as domain invariants, represent rules that reflect real-world fitness
behavior. It is important to identify and model these invariants to ensure consistency 
with how workouts, attendance, and progress naturally occur.

Within the fitness tracking domain, workout sessions represent bounded periods of
physical activity. A fundamental constraint is that every workout session must have a positive
duration. A session with zero or negative duration does not correspond to any meaningful real-
world activity. This invariant ensures that only valid workout sessions can exist within the
domain.

∀ session ∈ WorkoutHistory : session.duration > 0

This constraint directly influences domain operations such as recording or updating a
workout session. Any operation that attempts to create or modify a session must verify that the
duration is strictly positive. If this condition is not satisfied, the operation cannot produce a
valid domain state.

Another essential constraint is that every workout session must be associated with a
specific point in time. Workouts occur at identifiable moments, and without temporal
information, it is not possible to reason about order, frequency, or patterns of activity.

∀ session ∈ WorkoutHistory : ∃ day ∈ Day : session.day = day

This invariant ensures that sessions can be grouped into periods such as days or weeks,
which is necessary for evaluating consistency and generating summaries. As a result, all domain
operations must include temporal information when creating or modifying workout sessions.

Workout sessions are also inherently individual activities. Each session belongs to exactly
one person and cannot be shared across multiple individuals. This reflects the fact that fitness
progress and consistency are personal phenomena.

∀ session ∈ WorkoutHistory : ∃! person ∈ Person : owns(person, session)

This constraint enforces clear ownership of workout data and prevents ambiguity when
analyzing progress. It influences the design of the domain model by requiring that workout
histories are maintained per individual rather than globally.

Streaks represent another important domain concept and are governed by strict
constraints. A streak exists only when workout sessions occur on consecutive days without
interruption. The presence of a missed day breaks the streak.

∀ d₁, d₂ ∈ AttendanceDays : consecutive(d₁, d₂) ⇒ noMissedDaysBetween(d₁, d₂)

This invariant reflects the real-world interpretation of streaks as uninterrupted sequences.
It directly impacts operations that compute or update streak values, requiring them to detect
gaps in attendance and reset the streak accordingly.

In contrast, the absence of activity is also meaningful. A missed day represents a break in
an otherwise continuous pattern of attendance. This constraint ensures that inactivity is treated
as a significant domain phenomenon rather than being ignored.

∀ day ∈ Calendar : ¬∃ session ∈ WorkoutHistory : session.day = day ⇒ missed(day)

This invariant influences how consistency is evaluated, as both presence and absence of
activity contribute to understanding behavioral patterns over time.

Goals within the fitness tracking domain are defined over bounded time periods. A goal
must always be associated with a specific interval, such as a day or a week, and its evaluation is
only meaningful within that interval.

∀ goal ∈ Goals : ∃ period ∈ TimePeriod : definedOver(goal, period)

This constraint ensures that goal evaluation is context-specific and prevents ambiguous
interpretations of success. Domain operations that evaluate goals must therefore consider both.

Finally, consistency itself is not an isolated event but an emergent property derived from
repeated activity over time. A single workout session does not establish consistency.

consistency = f(WorkoutHistory, TimePeriod)

This reflects the domain understanding that consistency must be evaluated across multiple
sessions rather than inferred from individual events. As a result, domain operations must support
aggregation and analysis over historical data.

Together, these invariants define the boundaries within which valid domain states can
exist. They guide the design of domain operations by enforcing validation rules, shaping how
data is structured, and ensuring that derived concepts such as streaks and consistency are
computed correctly. By explicitly modeling these constraints, the domain description remains
faithful to real-world fitness behavior while providing a solid foundation for subsequent system
design and implementation.
