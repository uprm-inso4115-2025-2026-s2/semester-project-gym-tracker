= Lecture Topic Task: Requirements Stakeholders - Streak Feature User Personas
:author: Oscar Santana

== 🎯 Objective

Identify and document different stakeholder groups for the streak feature, defining their specific needs, goals, and concerns to ensure the streak system serves diverse user types effectively.

---

== 1. Stakeholder Context

The streak feature is designed for the Gym Tracker app's primary user base:
- College students
- Beginner and intermediate gym-goers
- Casual athletes
- Individuals building healthier habits

These users interact with core streak components:
- Current streak counter
- Grace period system (2-day buffer)
- Valid workout day definition (1 exercise / 5+ minutes)
- Longest streak tracking
- Streak reset conditions

---

== 2. Identified User Personas

=== Persona 1: The Anxious Beginner

*Background:* Sarah, 22, college student who just started going to the gym. Intimidated by fitness culture and fears failure.

*Goals:*
- Build consistent workout habit
- Stay motivated without feeling overwhelmed
- Not be punished for occasional missed days

*Frustrations:*
- Fear of losing streak after one missed day
- Anxiety about "perfect" attendance records
- Comparison to more experienced gym-goers

*Feature Needs:*
- Forgiving grace period
- Encouragement over punishment
- Low barrier to maintain streak (5-min minimum)
- Fresh start feeling after breaks

*Success Criteria:*
- Feels supported, not shamed
- Returns after missing days instead of quitting
- Sees streak as motivational, not stressful

---

=== Persona 2: The Busy Professional

*Background:* Marcus, 28, works irregular hours with unpredictable schedule. Wants to stay fit but often forgets to log workouts.

*Goals:*
- Track consistency despite chaotic schedule
- Recover from forgotten logging
- Maintain streak during travel/busy periods

*Frustrations:*
- Forgets to log workout until next day
- Loses streaks to technical issues (app crashes, sync failures)
- Travel across timezones disrupts tracking

*Feature Needs:*
- 48-hour backdating window
- Manual workout entry option
- Timezone-aware day boundaries
- Grace period for missed days

*Success Criteria:*
- Can fix forgotten logs without penalty
- Streak survives occasional chaos
- System accommodates real-life unpredictability

---

=== Persona 3: The Competitive Athlete

*Background:* Jake, 24, trains 6 days/week and values discipline. Sees streaks as accountability tool.

*Goals:*
- Track true consistency without exceptions
- Achieve long streaks (100+, 365+ days)
- Have permanent record of achievements

*Frustrations:*
- Grace periods feel like "cheating"
- Losing long streak to technical error is devastating
- Wants stricter rules than casual users

*Feature Needs:*
- Longest streak tracking (never resets)
- Transparent reset conditions
- Optional strict mode (no grace period)
- Milestone celebrations (30, 100, 365 days)

*Success Criteria:*
- Feels proud of earned streak
- Trust that streak count is accurate
- Personal records preserved forever

---

=== Persona 4: The Returning User

*Background:* Lisa, 26, had 60-day streak but stopped for 3 months due to injury. Now wants to restart.

*Goals:*
- Rebuild habit without dwelling on past failure
- Feel welcomed back, not shamed
- See that past effort still matters

*Frustrations:*
- Comparing new streak (3 days) to old streak (60 days) is demoralizing
- Fear of starting from zero again
- Guilt about losing previous progress

*Feature Needs:*
- Longest streak still visible (past achievement counts)
- Fresh start without penalty
- Optional one-time streak repair
- Total workouts tracked separately from streak

*Success Criteria:*
- Doesn't abandon app after break
- Sees returning as positive, not failure
- Past achievements acknowledged

---

=== Persona 5: The Perfectionist

*Background:* Emily, 23, obsesses over maintaining streak. Exercises when sick/injured to avoid reset.

*Goals:*
- Never lose streak
- Maintain "perfect" record
- Achieve longest possible streak

*Frustrations:*
- Anxiety about upcoming busy days
- Exercises during illness to maintain streak
- Can't enjoy rest days without guilt

*Feature Needs:*
- Scheduled rest days (mark in advance, don't break streak)
- Pause feature for illness/injury
- Reassurance that rest is healthy
- Separation of streak from workout quality

*Success Criteria:*
- Can take healthy breaks without anxiety
- Understands rest days are part of fitness
- Streak motivates without causing harm

---

== 3. Conflicting Requirements Analysis

=== Conflict 1: Forgiveness vs. Accountability

*Conflict:*
- Beginners need grace period to avoid discouragement
- Competitive users want strict rules without exceptions

*Current Resolution:*
- 2-day grace period balances both needs
- Not too strict (allows 2 missed days) but not too loose (resets on day 3)
- Longest streak tracking satisfies competitive users

*Trade-off:* Grace period feels "too easy" for competitive users, but is necessary for beginner retention

---

=== Conflict 2: Backdating vs. Integrity

*Conflict:*
- Professionals need backdating for forgotten logs
- Competitive users see backdating as "cheating"

*Current Resolution:*
- 48-hour backdating window limits abuse
- Manual entry allowed but time-restricted
- System trusts users (honor system)

*Trade-off:* Possible to game system, but benefits honest users more than it enables dishonesty

---

=== Conflict 3: Longest Streak Display vs. Discouragement

*Conflict:*
- Competitive users want longest streak prominently shown (achievement)
- Returning users find comparison to past discouraging (3 days vs. 60 days)

*Current Resolution:*
- Longest streak tracked but NOT on main screen
- Current streak emphasized over past record
- Total workouts shown alongside streak (alternative achievement)

*Trade-off:* Competitive users don't get prominent display, but returning users protected from demoralization

---

=== Conflict 4: Rest Days vs. Daily Consistency

*Conflict:*
- Perfectionists need rest days to not break streak (prevent unhealthy behavior)
- Competitive users see rest days as "skipping" (want true daily consistency)

*Current Resolution:*
- Rest days not implemented yet (deferred to future)
- Current system requires genuine activity
- Grace period provides some flexibility

*Unresolved Tension:* Perfectionists may exercise when sick to maintain streak (potential harm)

---

== 4. Feature-to-Stakeholder Mapping

=== Core Features Currently Implemented

*Grace Period (2-day buffer):*
- Primary: Anxious Beginner (prevents abandonment)
- Secondary: Busy Professional (accommodates chaotic schedules)
- Opposed by: Competitive Athlete (wants strict rules)
 
*48-Hour Backdating:*
- Primary: Busy Professional (fix forgotten logs)
- Secondary: Anxious Beginner (technical error recovery)
- Tolerated by: Competitive Athlete (if time-limited)
 
*Longest Streak Tracking:*
- Primary: Competitive Athlete (achievement recognition)
- Secondary: Returning User (past progress acknowledged)
- Neutral: Anxious Beginner (doesn't care about records yet)
 
*Valid Workout Definition (1 exercise OR 5 min):*
- Primary: Anxious Beginner (low barrier to entry)
- Secondary: Busy Professional (quick session counts)
- Neutral: Competitive Athlete (works out longer anyway)
 
*Total Workouts Counter:*
- Primary: Returning User (alternative achievement metric)
- Secondary: all personas (never resets, always growing)

---

=== Features Not Yet Implemented

*Scheduled Rest Days:*
- Primary beneficiary: Perfectionist (reduce anxiety)
- Secondary: Competitive Athlete (plan recovery properly)
- Implementation complexity: Medium (requires advance marking)

*Streak Repair (One-time recovery):*
- Primary: Returning User (recover from long break)
- Secondary: Anxious Beginner (fix early mistakes)
- Implementation complexity: Medium (requires usage tracking)

*Strict Mode Toggle:*
- Primary: Competitive Athlete (disable grace period)
- Opposed by: Anxious Beginner (would choose forgiving mode)
- Implementation complexity: High (dual logic paths)

== ✅ Conclusion

This stakeholder analysis shows that:

- Five distinct personas interact with streak feature differently
- Primary users (Anxious Beginner, Busy Professional) are well-served
- Conflicting requirements exist between forgiveness and strictness
- Current design prioritizes beginner retention over competitive features
- Perfectionist needs require attention to prevent unhealthy behaviors

The implemented features align with target demographic needs, though future iterations should address rest day scheduling and optional strict mode.
