==== [LTT] Domain Engineering Overview and Scope Boundary Refinement
:author: Pedro Cortes

This Lecture Topic Task applies domain engineering concepts to Gym Tracker by
clarifying the project's core domain focus and refining its scope boundaries.
Rather than analyzing one narrow feature, this task looks at the domain as a
whole and asks what the project is fundamentally designed to support, which
concepts are central to that purpose, and which related areas are intentionally
left outside the current scope.

Domain engineering benefits from a clear boundary because ambiguous scope tends
to spread into requirements, UI expectations, and future feature decisions. In
Gym Tracker, several elicitation statements already point toward a focused
domain centered on workout continuity, lightweight tracking, and progress
interpretation over time. The purpose of this LTT is to make that focus more
explicit and easier to defend.

===== 1. Core Domain Focus

Gym Tracker is fundamentally designed to support *workout consistency over
time*. The project is not simply about storing isolated exercise records. Its
main value comes from helping a GymGoer understand whether they are continuing
to show up, maintain habits, and make progress across days and weeks.

The most central question in the domain is therefore:

*How can a GymGoer track workout activity in a lightweight way that still
supports reflection, accountability, and progress awareness?*

This framing keeps the domain centered on behavior over time rather than on
maximizing detail for every possible fitness-related activity.

===== 2. In-Scope Concepts

The following concepts are central to the current Gym Tracker domain:

* *Workout Session* as the core recorded activity occurrence
* *Workout History* as the accumulated record of sessions across time
* *Attendance Day* and *Missed Day* as derived day-level participation concepts
* *Consistency* as the broader pattern of regular workout participation
* *Streak* as the narrower pattern of consecutive active days
* *Goal* and *Time Frame* as the basis for evaluating planned activity
* *Weekly Summary* and *Progress* as structured feedback over time

These concepts belong together because they all contribute to the same domain
purpose: helping a GymGoer log activity and interpret continuity, discipline,
and improvement.

===== 3. Scope Boundary Refinement

To keep the domain precise, it is necessary to distinguish *domain-central*
concepts from *domain-adjacent* concepts.

Domain-central concepts are those required to support workout logging,
consistency tracking, streak interpretation, and goal-based progress feedback.
Domain-adjacent concepts are related to fitness, but they introduce a broader
problem space than the current project is intended to address.

This refinement matters because relatedness alone is not enough to justify
inclusion in the current scope.

===== 4. Explicitly Out-of-Scope Areas

The following areas are related to fitness but intentionally outside the
current scope of Gym Tracker:

* *Nutrition tracking*
* *Full health monitoring*
* *Comprehensive wellness-platform behavior*
* *Detailed biometric analysis beyond workout-centered progress support*

These areas are excluded because they shift the project away from workout
consistency and toward a much broader health-management platform.

For example:

* *Nutrition tracking* introduces meals, calorie accounting, nutrient goals,
  and food logging workflows.
* *Full health monitoring* introduces sleep, recovery, body composition,
  medical indicators, and broader physiological metrics.
* *All-in-one wellness functionality* expands the project into a generalized
  lifestyle-management product rather than a focused workout tracker.

These concepts may be valid future extensions, but they are not necessary for
the present domain identity.

===== 5. Why These Boundaries Improve the Domain

Keeping the scope centered on workout consistency improves the documentation in
several ways:

* It preserves conceptual cohesion across the domain model.
* It prevents unrelated fitness concerns from distorting requirements.
* It makes feature decisions easier to justify.
* It keeps the system aligned with the lightweight logging motivation that came
  out of elicitation.

Without this boundary, Gym Tracker could drift toward trying to solve too many
problems at once, which would weaken both the domain description and the
project's practical focus.

===== 6. Alignment with Existing Domain Material

This refined scope aligns with the current project documentation in several
important ways:

* The rough sketch emphasizes friction reduction, workout logging, and habit
  continuity rather than broad health analytics.
* The narrative centers on Workout Sessions, Workout History, Consistency,
  Streak, Goal evaluation, and Weekly Summary.
* The concept analysis already treats consistency, streaks, goals, and
  summaries as core abstractions derived from stakeholder input.

This means the refinement does not introduce a new direction; it makes the
existing domain direction more explicit.

===== Conclusion

Gym Tracker's domain is best understood as a workout-consistency and
progress-interpretation system. Its core purpose is to help GymGoers record
Workout Sessions, interpret patterns across time, and stay oriented around
streaks, goals, and summaries. Related areas such as nutrition tracking and
full health monitoring remain relevant to the broader fitness context, but they
are intentionally outside the current scope.

By clarifying this overview and refining the domain boundary, the project
documentation gains stronger focus and is less vulnerable to future scope
drift.
