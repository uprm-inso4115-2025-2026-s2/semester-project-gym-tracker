=== Goal Obstruction Analysis for Streak Reliability
:author: Ariana Rodriguez

The streak system within the fitness tracking domain depends on the accurate
classification of attendance, reliable workout history management, and consistent evaluation of
activity over time. Because streaks are derived concepts rather than directly recorded values,
their correctness depends on several underlying domain conditions remaining valid. Identifying
potential obstacles that may interfere with these conditions is necessary to preserve reliability
within the system.

One important obstacle involves invalid or null workout timestamps. Workout sessions
must always occur at identifiable points in time, since streak calculations rely on determining the
chronological order of attendance. If workout timestamps are missing or malformed, the system
cannot correctly evaluate continuity between workout days.

∃ session ∈ WorkoutHistory : session.timestamp = null

This obstacle may cause incorrect attendance classification, invalid streak calculations,
or failures when displaying historical workout data. To mitigate this issue, workout sessions
must be validated before being included in streak computations, and timestamps should always
follow a consistent format.

Another obstacle emerges when duplicate workout sessions are recorded for the same
calendar day. Since streaks are based on attendance rather than the number of workouts
performed, multiple sessions within the same day should not artificially extend streak values.

∃ s₁, s₂ ∈ WorkoutHistory :
s₁.day = s₂.day ∧ s₁ ≠ s₂

If this obstacle is not controlled, streak counts and consistency metrics may become
inflated beyond what actually occurred. Mitigation requires defining aggregation rules so that
multiple sessions within the same day contribute to only one Attendance Day.

Chronological ordering also represents a critical reliability requirement. Streaks are
defined through uninterrupted sequences of consecutive attendance days, workout
sessions must always be evaluated in temporal order.

¬orderedChronologically(WorkoutHistory)

If workout sessions are processed out of order, streak calculations may incorrectly reset
or continue despite gaps in attendance. To reduce this risk, all streak-related operations should
enforce chronological sorting before evaluating attendance continuity.

Timezone inconsistencies create another significant obstacle. Workout timestamps may
be interpreted differently across systems, devices, or locations, causing sessions to appear under
incorrect calendar days.

∃ session ∈ WorkoutHistory :
interpretedDay(session, timezone₁) ≠ interpretedDay(session, timezone₂)

This obstacle may unexpectedly break streaks even when workouts were completed on
consecutive real-world days. Mitigation requires defining standardized timezone handling and
consistent date conversion rules throughout the system.

Missing workout data also threatens streak reliability. Workout sessions may fail to save,
synchronize, or persist correctly due to storage or communication issues.

∃ session ∈ WorkoutHistory : ¬persisted(session)

If workout records are missing, streak calculations may incorrectly classify attended days
as missed days. This may result in unexpected streak loss and inaccurate consistency summaries.
To mitigate this obstacle, persistence validation and synchronization verification should occur
before streak updates are finalized.

Another obstacle concerns inconsistent attendance classification rules across different
system components. If attendance evaluation logic differs between services or views, the same
user activity may produce contradictory streak results.

∃ component₁, component₂ :
attendanceResult(component₁) ≠ attendanceResult(component₂)

This inconsistency may cause users to observe different streak values across the system,
reducing trust in the reliability of the application. Mitigation requires centralized attendance
classification rules and shared validation constraints across all streak-related operations.

Streaks themselves are governed by strict domain constraints. A streak only exists when
attendance occurs on consecutive days without interruption. A missed day immediately breaks
the streak under the current domain rules.

∀ d₁, d₂ ∈ AttendanceDays :
consecutive(d₁, d₂) ⇒ noMissedDaysBetween(d₁, d₂)

This constraint means that even small inconsistencies in workout history or attendance
classification may significantly affect streak calculations. Reliable streak evaluation therefore
depends on preserving accurate and complete attendance data over time.

Consistency within the domain is also an emergent property rather than an isolated event.
A single workout session does not establish meaningful consistency or streak behavior.

consistency = f(WorkoutHistory, AttendanceDays, TimePeriod)

As a result, the reliability of the streak system depends on maintaining valid historical
data and correctly evaluating activity patterns across multiple days rather than individual events.
Together, these obstacles demonstrate that streak reliability depends not only on streak
calculation logic itself, but also on the correctness of workout timestamps, attendance
classification, persistence behavior, and chronological consistency.
