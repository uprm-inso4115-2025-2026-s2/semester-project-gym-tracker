=== Temporal Analysis of Streak Continuity
:author: Ariana Rodriguez

The streak system depends on the continuous evaluation of attendance behavior 
across time. Streak continuity is not directly recorded as an isolated value, 
but instead from ordered workout history, attendance classification, inactivity periods, 
and temporal relationships between workout sessions. Because streak persistence depends on
multiple time-dependent conditions remaining valid simultaneously, analyzing the
temporal behavior governing streak continuity is necessary to preserve
consistency and reliability within the domain.

One important temporal relationship involves consecutive attendance days.
A streak only persists when qualifying workout activity occurs on consecutive
calendar days without interruption. The streak system evaluates continuity by
comparing workout dates and determining whether attendance occurred within the
expected temporal sequence.

This temporal relationship directly affects streak persistence because
continuity depends on uninterrupted attendance progression. If consecutive
attendance is not maintained, streak progression stops and inactivity handling
begins. To preserve continuity, workout sessions must always be evaluated in
chronological order using consistent date calculations.

Missed days introduce a new temporal behavior. A missed day occurs when 
no qualifying workout activity is recorded during a calendar day.
Because streak continuity depends on uninterrupted attendance, inactivity
affects the state of the streak system.

The first missed day activates the grace period state, while additional
missed days increase the risk of streak interruption. This behavior affects
streak continuity because prolonged inactivity causes streak reset conditions 
to occur. To mitigate premature interruption, the domain introduces a temporary recovery 
window through grace period handling.

Grace periods represent a temporal recovery mechanism intended to
preserve streak continuity during short inactivity periods. Instead of
immediately resetting the streak after one missed day, the system temporarily
maintains continuity while monitoring additional attendance behavior.

This temporal constraint allows users to recover continuity by resuming
qualifying workout activity before the grace period expires. The grace period
progresses through multiple temporal states, including warning, critical, and
expiration states depending on the number of consecutive missed days.

This behavior affects streak persistence because continuity may still be
restored during the grace period window. However, once inactivity exceeds the
allowed threshold, the streak resets.

Workout timing and calendar-day interpretation also influence streak
continuity. Attendance classification depends not only on whether a workout was
performed, but also on the local time at which workout activity occurred.

This temporal relationship becomes important near midnight boundaries or
during timezone transitions. If workout timestamps are interpreted
incorrectly, sessions may appear under different calendar days, unexpectedly
interrupting continuity. To preserve reliable streak behavior, workout sessions
must always be interpreted using standardized timezone calculations and local
midnight boundaries.

Another temporal behavior involves duplicate workouts within the same calendar
day. Because streaks are derived from attendance rather than workout quantity, 
multiple workout sessions within one day must not artificially
extend continuity.

This constraint prevents inflated streak progression and preserves
correct attendance classification. Multiple workouts within the same day still
contribute to only one Attendance Day within the continuity calculation.

Workout history ordering also represents a critical temporal constraint.
Streak persistence depends entirely on evaluating attendance in chronological
order across time.

If workout sessions are processed out of sequence, continuity calculations
may incorrectly continue or reset streaks despite actual attendance behavior.
To preserve reliability, workout history must always remain chronologically
sorted before streak calculations occur.

Backdating introduces another important temporal relationship. Users may
complete workouts but fail to log them immediately. To preserve continuity in
these situations, the domain allows workout entries to be added retroactively
within a limited temporal window.

This temporal rule supports continuity recovery while preventing abuse of
the streak system. Backdated workouts may restore interrupted attendance
sequences when entered within the allowed timeframe. However, entries beyond the
48-hour window are rejected to preserve integrity within continuity.

Streak reset behavior itself is also governed by temporal constraints. A
streak resets only after inactivity exceeds the permitted grace period.

This relationship connects inactivity duration to streak interruption. 
Once a reset occurs, the current streak value returns to zero while
historical records such as longest streak values remain preserved.

Both attendance history and activity patterns form the foundation of
streak continuity. Individual workouts alone do not establish meaningful streak
behavior. Instead, continuity emerges from repeated attendance across extended
time periods.

As a result, streak persistence depends on maintaining accurate workout
history, reliable attendance classification, valid temporal ordering, and
consistent inactivity handling across multiple days rather than isolated workout
events.
