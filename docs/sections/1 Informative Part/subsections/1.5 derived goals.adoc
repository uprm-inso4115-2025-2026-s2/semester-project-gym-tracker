=== *1.5 Derived Goals*

The primary objective of the project is to design a centralized system
that reduces friction in fitness tracking and supports consistency. [.changed]#This
need is plausible in the current tool landscape: some tools offer many
features but can feel excessive for a gym goer who mainly wants to record
workouts and review progress. For example, platforms such as MyFitnessPal
or Garmin Connect combine multiple dashboards, metrics, and tracking areas
that may be useful for advanced [.changed]#GymGoers# but also increase interaction
overhead. Other tools focus strongly on only one dimension of fitness, such
as Strava on endurance activity tracking or Strong on strength-training log
detail. These examples do not make such tools bad; they illustrate that the
project addresses a gap for [.changed]#GymGoers# who want a simpler and more balanced view
of attendance, progress, and routine.#

[.changed]#Beyond this primary objective, several secondary goals naturally arise from
addressing the identified domain problems. These derived goals are not
functional requirements, but broader outcomes that the project aims to
support.#

==== *Strengthening Long-Term Habit Formation*

A derived goal of the project is to strengthen long-term habit formation.
If [.changed]#GymGoers# consistently log workouts and observe their progress over time,
they are more likely to internalize exercise as part of their identity
rather than as a temporary activity.

[.changed]#This goal is distinct from merely recording workouts. It focuses on the
longer-term stabilization of exercise as part of a routine, rather than on
the immediate act of logging a session with low friction.#

==== *Encouraging Self-Reflection*

Another derived goal is to promote self-reflection regarding physical
effort, consistency, and improvement patterns. When individuals become
aware of their routines and gaps, they are better positioned to make
intentional decisions about their health and performance.

[.changed]#This goal extends beyond storing workout data. It supports awareness and
intentional evaluation of personal habits, for example by noticing whether
missed days cluster on certain weekdays or whether some workout types are
being neglected over time.#

==== *Supporting Personal Accountability*

The project also aims to support a culture of personal accountability.
By maintaining a visible history of actions over time, individuals
confront objective records of their consistency.

[.changed]#This is not a system feature, but a behavioral outcome: encouraging GymGoers
to take responsibility for their commitments by making their own patterns
harder to ignore.#

==== [.changed]#*Making Tracking Feel Sustainable Rather Than Punitive*#

[.changed]#Related to the primary goal of reducing friction, a secondary goal is to
make tracking feel sustainable over time rather than punitive after missed
days or imperfect weeks. Many [.changed]#GymGoers# do not stop tracking only because the
steps are inconvenient, but also because the experience can amplify guilt
or make lapses feel like failure.#

[.changed]#This goal is therefore narrower than the primary goal. The primary goal is
to reduce the overall effort of tracking; this secondary goal concerns the
kind of experience created around that tracking, especially after breaks,
mistakes, or inconsistent periods.#

==== *Promoting a Growth-Oriented Mindset*

A final derived goal is to promote a growth-oriented mindset in which
progress is evaluated over time rather than judged in isolated sessions.
This encourages [.changed]#GymGoers# to value gradual improvement instead of perfection.

[.changed]#This partly complements the goal of supporting consistency, but it is not
identical to it. Consistency is about whether activity is maintained; a
growth-oriented mindset is about how that activity is interpreted. A person
may remain consistent yet still evaluate themselves harshly, or may break a
streak and still continue constructively if progress is framed over a
longer horizon.#
